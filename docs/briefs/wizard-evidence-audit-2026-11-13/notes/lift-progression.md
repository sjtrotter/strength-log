# Lift progression, effort and estimation rules: what the primary literature supports

Scope: lift rows in `inventory.md` that cover progression, effort, loading, rest or estimation. Priority rows are L30, L37, L40, L28, L25, rep ranges and RIR targets, rest (L49 to L53), the two-miss drop (L34) and the light week (L54 to L57). Abstracts were read through Europe PMC. Full texts were read for ACSM 2009, ACSM 2026, Nuzzo 2024, Helms 2016, Helms 2018 and Macarilla 2022. Lines marked "Epley arithmetic" were computed here from Epley's formula, e1RM = w × (1 + reps/30). Those numbers are a model, not data, and the arithmetic sits under the matching rule's Inferences.

Populations at a glance: most RIR-accuracy data come from young men and women aged 18 to 35, mostly trained. Most 1RM-equation data come from untrained college students, with a few studies of older adults and of trained athletes. The trained-progression data come from young trained men and from competitive powerlifters. Most of the ACSM 2026 evidence base is novices.

## Q1. What the best evidence says for each rule: population, effect size or accuracy, and verdict

### Takeaway
No study tests any of these exact numbers. Five rules sit inside a published convention or are now verified against a primary text: L37's two-success cadence and L41's 10% cap (both ACSM 2009, read in full), the 180 s strength rest (Grgic 2018, Schoenfeld 2016), the 10-rep Epley limit (Reynolds 2006, Mayhew 2008) and the light-week interval (Rogerson 2024). Three rules conflict with arithmetic built on the RIR-accuracy evidence. The ×1.25 calibration jump (L28) lands near or past failure unless the "Easy" set really had about 11 or more reps left. The novice Easy-session doubling (L42) overshoots in the same way. The 90% back-offs (L08) end about 6 reps short of failure. The 1.25% upper-body increment (L40) is below ACSM's 2% floor, but whole-plate rounding makes the gap mostly irrelevant.

### Cited Findings

**RIR self-report accuracy (L30, L32, L43, and the basis for L28 and L42)**
- Meta-analysis of 12 studies, 414 participants, 262 effect sizes. People **underpredict reps to failure by 0.95 reps (95% CI 0.17 to 1.73)**, with I² = 97.9% heterogeneity. Accuracy improves slightly closer to failure (β = −0.025 per rep) and in later sets (β = −0.07). It is far better on sets of 12 or fewer reps (β = 0.06, 95% CI 0.04 to 0.09) than on sets above 12 (β = 0.47, 95% CI 0.44 to 0.49). Training status had no effect (β = −0.006, 95% CI −0.02 to 0.007), and neither did upper versus lower body (−0.58 reps, 95% CI −2.32 to 1.16). Between-participant SD of accuracy was **1.45 reps (95% CI 0.99 to 2.12)**. Mixed trained and untrained healthy adults. — [Halperin et al. 2022, Sports Med, doi:10.1007/s40279-021-01559-x](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542869/)
- 24 resistance-trained adults (12 men, 12 women) on the bench press at 75% 1RM, calling 1 RIR and 3 RIR during sets taken to failure. Raw error was **−0.17 ± 1.00 reps** and absolute error **0.65 ± 0.78 reps**, with statistical equivalence (±1 rep) between 1-RIR and 3-RIR calls, set 1 and set 2, and session 1 and session 2. No relation to sex, years of experience or relative strength. — [Refalo et al. 2024, JSCR 38(3):e78–e85, doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000004653](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37967832/)
- 58 trained and untrained men and women on machine single-joint exercises at 72.5% 1RM. Calls got more accurate closer to failure and in later sets (p < 0.01). Sex (p = 0.917), training experience (p = 0.462) and RIR-rating experience had no effect. — [Remmert et al. 2023, Percept Mot Skills, doi:10.1177/00315125231169868](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37036795/)
- 25 trained men (training age 4.7 ± 3.2 years), squat at 70% 1RM, average 16 ± 4 reps to failure. Error grew with distance from failure: **2.05 ± 1.73 reps when calling 1 RIR, 3.65 ± 2.46 when calling 3 RIR, and 5.15 ± 2.92 when calling 5 RIR**. More reps per set predicted larger error at the 5 and 7 RPE calls. Training age did not. — [Zourdos et al. 2021, JSCR 35(2S):S158–S165, doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000002995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30747900/)
- 141 trainees, single sets to momentary failure. Everyone underpredicted, with an SEM of **2.64 to 3.38 reps**, and accuracy "tended" to improve with experience. — [Steele et al. 2017, PeerJ 5:e4105, PMC5712461](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29204323/)
- At a true 1RM, novices under-rate effort. Squat: experienced lifters (5.2 years) rated it higher than novices (0.4 years), p = 0.023; Helms 2016 gives the SD of the experienced group's single-rep ratings as 0.32 at 100% and 1.18 at 60%. Bench: RPE 9.86 ± 0.14 for experienced benchers (4.7 years) versus 9.35 ± 0.36 for novices (1.1 years), p = 0.011. No group differences at 60 to 90%. College-aged men. — [Zourdos et al. 2016, JSCR 30(1):267–275, doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000001049](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26049792/); [Ormsbee et al. 2019, JSCR 33(2):337–345](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28301439/); [Helms et al. 2016, Strength Cond J 38(4):42–49, PMC4961270](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961270/)
- The RIR-based RPE scale: RPE 10 = 0 RIR, 9 = 1, 8 = 2, 7 = 3, and **5–6 grouped as "4–6 RIR"**. Scores 1–4 carry effort words ("light effort", "little to no effort") instead of rep counts, because "it is easier for athletes to give a range of RIR when RIR is greater than 3." Helms advises that novices "should practice recording RIR, but likely not base training intensity or progression solely on" the scale until their accuracy improves. — [Helms et al. 2016, PMC4961270](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961270/)
- ACSM 2026: "sufficient effort" is near failure or a target of **2–3 RIR**. However, "there is insufficient evidence to quantify exact RIR and perceived exertion targets." — [Currier et al. 2026, ACSM Position Stand, MSSE, doi:10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897, PMC12965823](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)
- No study was found that maps everyday effort words ("easy", "hard") to RIR. Validated anchors exist only for the RIR-RPE scale above and for OMNI-RES. The OMNI-RES work tested criterion RPEs of 4, 6 and 8 in adults aged 60 to 70 and did not relate them to RIR. — [Gearhart et al. 2009, JSCR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19387373/)

**Autoregulation versus fixed loading (context for the whole RIR engine)**
- 15 studies, resistance-trained people. Autoregulated (RIR-RPE or velocity) load versus percentage-based: 1RM difference **MD 2.07 kg (95% CI −0.32 to 4.46), p = 0.09, SMD 0.21**. — [Hickmott et al. 2022, Sports Med Open, PMC8762534](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35038063/)
- 21 trained men (at least 2 years; squat 141 ± 21 kg, bench 117 ± 19 kg), 8 weeks of daily undulating training, squat and bench 3×/week. RPE group versus %1RM group: squat **+17.05 ± 5.44 versus +13.91 ± 5.89 kg**, bench **+10.70 ± 3.30 versus +9.64 ± 5.36 kg**. Differences were non-significant, with a likely small advantage for RPE (ES about 0.5 on squat). — [Helms et al. 2018, Front Physiol, PMC5877330](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5877330/)
- 14 studies, 356 participants. Every autoregulation protocol raised 1RM, with small to large ES. — [Larsen et al. 2021, PeerJ, PMC7810043](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33520457/)

**Load progression rules: cadence (L37) and increments (L39, L40, L41)**
- ACSM 2009, read in full: "a 2–10% (lower percent for small muscle mass exercises, higher percent increase for large muscle mass exercises) increase in load be applied when the individual can perform the current workload for one to two repetitions over the desired number **on two consecutive training sessions**." It is graded Evidence category B and cites Feigenbaum & Pollock 1999. The rule is stated for novice, intermediate and advanced lifters alike. — [ACSM 2009 Position Stand, MSSE 41:687–708, doi:10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181915670](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/) (full text read from a [hosted copy](https://sfu.ca/~ryand/kin343/ACSMresistance.pdf))
- ACSM 2026 sets no increment or cadence. Progression can come from raising relative load, or from holding the same relative load while "perceived exertion scales are used to increase absolute load commensurate with strength gains." It adds that "progression is not necessary to achieve beneficial outcomes," and that overload is "likely a requirement only for those seeking continued longer term progress." — [Currier et al. 2026, PMC12965823](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)
- In 1,896 powerlifters, maximum yearly gains were **squat 20.2–25.4 kg, deadlift 18.1–21.1 kg and bench 10.5–12.8 kg**, with no sex difference. These are absolute kilograms. — [Latella et al. 2022, MSSE, doi:10.1249/MSS.0000000000002858](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35019902/)
- In 1,897 powerlifters over 15 years, the strongest male quartile gained **0.102 kg/day versus 0.211 kg/day** for the least strong. — [Latella et al. 2020, JSCR, PMC7448836](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32865942/)
- 14,690 adults (60% women, aged 48 ± 11) on 1×/week single sets to failure: strength followed a linear-log curve, gaining about 30–50% in year one and reaching a practical plateau by 1–2 years. — [Steele et al. 2023, Res Q Exerc Sport, doi:10.1080/02701367.2022.2070592](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35591809/)
- 43 lifters with at least 1 year of training, 8 weeks: progressing load versus progressing reps gave similar results. Smith-machine squat strength slightly favored load (+2.0 kg, 90% CI −2.4 to 7.8). — [Plotkin et al. 2022, PeerJ, PMC9528903](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36199287/)
- The NSCA "2-for-2" rule (add load after two or more extra reps on the last set in two consecutive sessions) and its increments (2.5–5 lb upper and 5–10 lb lower for smaller or less-trained athletes; 5–10 lb upper and 10–15 lb lower for larger or more-trained athletes) appear only in exam-prep summaries of the NSCA textbook. The primary text was not available. — [Achievable CSCS](https://app.achievable.me/study/cscs/learn/program-design-for-resistance-training-training-frequency-exercise-order-and-training-load-and-repetitions) (secondary)

**Calibration jump (L28, L29)**
- No study tests a jump size for finding a working load. The nearest convention is the NSCA 1RM-test warm-up: from a light 5–10-rep set, add **5–10% (10–20 lb) for upper body or 10–20% (30–40 lb) for lower body** to reach a 3–5-rep set, then the same again to reach a 2–3-rep set. After a failed attempt, remove 2.5–5% for upper body or 5–10% for lower body. — [HPRC (US DoD), attributing the protocol to the NSCA](https://www.hprc-online.org/articles/one-rep-max-for-strength) (secondary to the NSCA textbook)

**1RM estimation, Epley trusted to 10 reps (L25)**
- 70 adults (34 men, 36 women) aged 18–69, chest press and leg press, tested at 1, 5, 10 and 20RM. The 5RM predicted 1RM best (leg press R² 0.974, 0.933 and 0.915 for 5, 10 and 20RM; chest press 0.993, 0.976 and 0.955). The load–reps relationship was nonlinear. The authors conclude "no more than 10 repetitions should be used in linear equations to estimate 1RM." — [Reynolds et al. 2006, JSCR 20(3):584–592, doi:10.1519/R-15304.1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16937972/)
- 103 untrained young women, bench press at 60–90% 1RM, before and after 12 weeks of training. Equations "were more accurate ... in which fewer than 10 RTF [repetitions to fatigue] were used." Reps at a fixed %1RM changed unpredictably with training (individual change 27 ± 99%). — [Mayhew et al. 2008, JSCR, doi:10.1519/JSC.0b013e31817b02ad](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18714230/)
- LeSuer 1997: 67 untrained college students, seven equations, bench, squat and deadlift. Every equation **underestimated deadlift 1RM by 22.2–33.7 lb (9–14%)** despite high correlations. Only Mayhew and Wathan predicted the bench, and only Wathan the squat. These figures come from a peer-reviewed secondary citation; the original was not read. — [LeSuer et al. 1997, JSCR 11(4):211–213, doi:10.1519/00124278-199711000-00001](https://doi.org/10.1519/00124278-199711000-00001), as reported in [Eston & Evans 2009, J Sports Sci Med, PMC3761544](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3761544/)
- DiStasio 2014, a thesis on Division I football players: Epley predicted back-squat 1RM within **2.7 kg** and Brzycki within 3.1 kg, from 5RM and 3RM tests. — reported in [Macarilla et al. 2022, J Hum Kinet, PMC9465738](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9465738/) (secondary; the thesis was not read)
- In older adults, Wood 2002 is cited for better equation accuracy at 10 or fewer reps. The DOI was verified but the text was not read. — [Wood et al. 2002, Meas Phys Educ Exerc Sci 6(2):67–94, doi:10.1207/s15327841mpee0602_1](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327841mpee0602_1). Tan 2015 built separate 1RM equations from 4–10RM and 11–15RM in 109 adults aged 60–75 (SEE 1.08–5.88). — [Tan et al. 2015, J Aging Phys Act](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25594489/)
- Nuzzo 2024 meta-regression, full text read: 952 reps-to-failure tests, 7,289 people, 269 studies; 60% trained, 66% male. Main model: about **5 reps at 90% and about 15 at 70%**. Bench press: about **4, 9 and 14 reps at 90, 80 and 70%**. Leg press: about **9, 13 and 19**. The between-individual **SD of reps is 2.51 at 80% 1RM and 4.36 at 60%**. Sex, age and training status had little influence; exercise mattered (leg press allows more reps than bench). The textbook table it replaces lists 90% → 4, 80% → 8 and 70% → 11. — [Nuzzo et al. 2024, Sports Med 54:303–321, doi:10.1007/s40279-023-01937-7, PMC10933212](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933212/)

**Rep ranges and RIR targets per goal (L05, L06, L10 to L17)**
- ACSM 2026: strength improves with **≥80% 1RM**, 2–3 sets, and at least 2 sessions a week. Hypertrophy improves with ≥10 sets/week, and load (30% versus 100%) did not change it. Training to failure "did not consistently impact" outcomes. — [Currier et al. 2026, PMC12965823](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)
- ACSM 2009: novices 8–12RM (60–70% 1RM). Intermediate and advanced lifters 1–12RM, with emphasis on 1–6RM for strength and 6–12RM for hypertrophy. — [ACSM 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/)
- Exploratory meta-regressions with RIR estimated from study descriptions: strength gain is flat across a wide RIR range, while hypertrophy rises as sets end closer to failure. — [Robinson et al. 2024, Sports Med, doi:10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/)
- 15 studies: failure versus non-failure for hypertrophy gave a trivial ES of 0.19 (95% CI 0.00 to 0.37) for any definition of failure, and ES 0.12 (−0.13 to 0.37, non-significant) for momentary failure. — [Refalo et al. 2023, Sports Med, PMC9935748](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/)

**Rest (L49 to L53, L76)**
- Systematic review, 23 studies, 491 participants: robust strength gains even with rests under 60 s, but **"longer duration RIs (> 2 min) are required to maximize strength gains in resistance-trained individuals"**. For untrained people, 60–120 s is sufficient. — [Grgic et al. 2018, Sports Med 48:137–151, doi:10.1007/s40279-017-0788-x](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28933024/)
- 21 trained young men, 8 weeks, 3 × 8–12RM: 3-min rest beat 1-min rest for squat and bench 1RM and for thigh muscle thickness. — [Schoenfeld et al. 2016, JSCR 30(7):1805–1812](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26605807/)
- Bayesian meta-analysis, 9 studies (19 measurements), mostly untrained: a small hypertrophy benefit from rests longer than 60 s, and "no appreciable differences ... when resting >90 s." — [Singer et al. 2024, Front Sports Act Living, PMC11349676](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/)
- ACSM 2009: rest **at least 2–3 min** for core exercises with heavier loads (category B) and 1–2 min for assistance exercises (category C). For hypertrophy, 1–2 min for novices and intermediates. — [ACSM 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/)
- ACSM 2026: strength was not affected by inter-set rest under versus over 1 minute. Most of that evidence comes from novices. — [Currier et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)

**Light weeks and deloads (L54 to L57)**
- Survey of 246 competitive strength and physique athletes (8.2 ± 6.2 years of training): deloads lasted **6.4 ± 1.7 days, every 5.6 ± 2.3 weeks**. Athletes cut reps and sets, cut load, raised RIR, and kept frequency and exercise selection. Deloads were planned, autoregulated, or triggered by stalled performance or joint aches. — [Rogerson et al. 2024, Sports Med Open, PMC10948666](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38499934/)
- 39 trained young adults: a 1-week **full stop** at the midpoint of 9 weeks gave smaller lower-body strength gains and no hypertrophy benefit. — [Coleman et al. 2024, PeerJ, PMC10809978](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38274324/)

**Misses and resets (L33 to L36)**
- No trial tests a reset size or a miss count. In a popular novice program, the convention is to deload after **three** failures in a row by **about 10%**, never after a single failed workout. — [StrongLifts 5×5, "How to Overcome Failure"](https://stronglifts.com/stronglifts-5x5/failure/) (program author, convention source)

**Layoff (L58 to L61), for context; detraining is mainly another researcher's scope**
- Meta-analysis of 103 studies: stopping training lowers maximal force (SMD −0.46, 95% CI −0.54 to −0.37), with a dose-response by duration and larger effects in people over 65. The "no loss up to 28 days" figure does not appear in the abstract. — [Bosquet et al. 2013, Scand J Med Sci Sports](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23347054/)
- 20 trained men: leg-press strength held after 2 weeks of detraining. — [Hwang et al. 2017, JSCR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28328712/)

### Inferences

Epley arithmetic: the load per rep of reserve is load(RTF n) ÷ load(RTF n+1). It comes to 2.9% at 5 reps to failure, 2.6% at 8, 2.5% at 10 and 2.2% at 15. Each rep of RIR is worth about 2.5% of load in the 5–12 rep range.

From a true 4 RIR it leaves the lifter about 4–5 reps *short of the target*; from a true 6 RIR, about 2–3 reps short. Calibration still ends correctly, because the next set at ≤3 RIR is run through Epley. The cost is a failed or grinding set, often by a New lifter under a barbell, which conflicts with L18's "never below 2 RIR." The two-step floor makes a 10 lb dumbbell jump to 20 lb (+100%). Low-rep calls by attentive lifters are accurate within about 1 rep (Refalo), so a true 4–6 is common at the end of calibration. On high-rep sets "Easy" is often really 8–10 (Zourdos 2021), which is where ×1.25 fits. |

| L43 | Max set blocks a load increase | **KEEP as PRACTICE** | Zero reported RIR leaves no margin. |
| L44 | Double progression | **KEEP as PRACTICE** | Plotkin 2022: rep progression is about as effective as load progression in trained lifters. |
| L45, L46 | Coarse step +4 reps; bodyweight to 20 reps | **LABEL AS PRACTICE** | RIR accuracy collapses above 12 reps (Halperin β 0.47; Zourdos 2021), so bodyweight sets near 20 should progress on reps, not on RIR. They already do. |
| **L49** | Strength MAIN 180 s | **KEEP; now SUPPORTED** | Grgic 2018 (>2 min maximizes strength in trained lifters), Schoenfeld 2016 (3 min > 1 min in trained men), ACSM 2009 (at least 2–3 min for core lifts). ACSM 2026 found no strength effect in mostly novice data. |
| L50–L52 | 120 s / 90 s / 60 s | **KEEP** | Singer 2024: no hypertrophy difference beyond 90 s, slight benefit above 60 s. ACSM 2009: 1–2 min for assistance work. |
| L53, L76 | RAMP 90 s, BACKOFF 120 s; MAIN rest capped at 120 s in short sessions | **LABEL AS PRACTICE** | The 120 s cap is inside Grgic's sufficient range for untrained lifters. For trained strength lifters it is a stated trade-off. |
| **L54–L57** | Light cycle: 60% of sets, ×0.9 load, one rotation long, every 6 rotations (4–8), plus reactive triggers | **KEEP as PRACTICE** | Rogerson 2024: about 6.4 days every 5.6 ± 2.3 weeks, cutting sets, reps and load and raising RIR; stalls and joint aches as triggers. Every 6 rotations matches about 6 weeks only when a rotation lasts about a week. Coleman 2024 found a full stop costs strength, which supports a light week rather than a week off. |

### Gaps
- No study validates everyday effort words against measured reps to failure, so the L30 mapping can only be practice. The app's own logs (rated sets followed by a rep miss) are the only way to calibrate it.
- No study tests a calibration-jump size or a miss-and-reset rule. The NSCA protocol and the StrongLifts rules are conventions found through secondary or program-author sources; the NSCA *Essentials* textbook was not available.
- LeSuer 1997, Wood 2002 and the DiStasio thesis were not read in the original. Their numbers come from peer-reviewed secondary citations (Eston 2009, Macarilla 2022).
- Graham & Cleather 2021's 10.8% versus 7.1% squat result: the abstract was empty in Europe PMC, so it was not re-verified here.
- The "40% untrained ... 2% elite" average strength gains are **not in the ACSM 2009 full text**. The likely sources are ACSM 2002 or Kraemer & Ratamess 2004, neither of which was checked.
- Nuzzo's full per-%1RM table is in figures. Only the values quoted in the text (90, 80, 70 and 60%) were extracted.
- Latella 2022 baseline loads were not obtained, so their per-lift gains cannot be turned into percentages.

## Q2. Where evidence is absent, what do established programs and position stands use, and are our numbers inside that convention?

### Takeaway
Most of the app's numbers sit inside ACSM 2009 conventions: the 2–10% increment band, two consecutive sessions, rest of at least 2–3 min on core lifts and 1–2 min on assistance work, novice loads of 8–12RM, and the ≥80% heavy load ACSM 2026 sets for strength. The exceptions are the 1.25% upper increment (below ACSM's 2%), the ×1.25 calibration jump with its two-step floor (above NSCA's 5–10% upper-body warm-up step), and the 2-miss, 5% reset (gentler and earlier than StrongLifts' 3-miss, 10% reset).

### Cited Findings
- ACSM 2009: 2–10% increments, smaller for small muscle masses, applied after 1–2 reps over target on two consecutive sessions. Rest of at least 2–3 min for core lifts and 1–2 min for assistance work. Novices at 8–12RM, 60–70% 1RM. — [ACSM 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/)
- ACSM 2026: ≥80% 1RM, 2–3 sets, ≥2 sessions a week for strength; 2–3 RIR as sufficient effort; "insufficient evidence to quantify exact RIR ... targets"; progression via perceived-exertion scales is acceptable. — [Currier et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)
- NSCA 1RM warm-up protocol: +5–10% upper and +10–20% lower between warm-up sets; −2.5–5% upper and −5–10% lower after a failed attempt. — [HPRC](https://www.hprc-online.org/articles/one-rep-max-for-strength)
- NSCA 2-for-2 rule and absolute increments, from exam-prep summaries only. — [Achievable CSCS](https://app.achievable.me/study/cscs/learn/program-design-for-resistance-training-training-frequency-exercise-order-and-training-load-and-repetitions)
- StrongLifts 5×5: 5 lb steps, microloading on the presses, deload about 10% after three failed workouts, 3–5 min rest on heavy sets. — [StrongLifts](https://stronglifts.com/stronglifts-5x5/failure/)
- Competitive athletes deload about every 5.6 weeks for about 6.4 days. — [Rogerson et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38499934/)

### Inferences
- L37's cadence 2 *is* ACSM's rule. The inventory's INVENTED label should become PRACTICE (ACSM 2009, category B). The novice cadence of 1 is the faster novice-program convention.
- L41's 10% cap is exactly ACSM's ceiling.
- L40's 1.25% upper sits below ACSM's 2% floor, while the 2.5% lower is inside the band.
- L28's ×1.25 sits above NSCA's upper-body warm-up step and at the top of its lower-body range. Its two-step minimum has no counterpart.
- L34's 5% after two misses is gentler and earlier than StrongLifts' 10% after three. Both are untested.
- L49–L52 rests sit within ACSM 2009 and the meta-analyses.
- L56's light week every 6 rotations (4–8) sits within Rogerson's 5.6 ± 2.3 weeks.

### Gaps
- No position stand gives a calibration protocol for a novice's first working load, a miss counter, or a reset size.

## Q3. Which rules, if wrong, would most mislead a real user?

### Takeaway
The highest-risk rules are L28 (calibration ×1.25 with a two-step floor) and L30 (the effort-word mapping, with no word for 3 RIR). Both affect every New lifter's first sessions and every rated set. L42's novice Easy doubling and L08's light back-offs come next. L40 and L25 matter less than their inventory rank suggests.

### Cited Findings
- On low-rep sets, trained raters are accurate within about 0.65 reps. — [Refalo et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37967832/)
- Reports far from failure are off by about 5 reps on high-rep sets. — [Zourdos et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30747900/)
- Novices under-rate effort near a true max. — [Zourdos et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26049792/); [Ormsbee et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28301439/)
- Strength is insensitive to RIR across a wide range, so an under-dosed back-off costs little strength. — [Robinson et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/)
- Nuzzo: between-individual SD of 2.51 reps at 80% 1RM. — [Nuzzo et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933212/)

### Inferences
1. **L28 calibration.** Epley says a true 4–6-RIR "Easy" set followed by ×1.25 leaves the lifter 2–5 reps short of target. A New lifter calibrating a barbell squat or bench gets a failed or grinding set during the very session the plan promises to keep at 3 RIR or more. The two-step floor doubles a light dumbbell. The cost is safety and affect in the first month, which the earlier review flagged as the window that decides retention.
2. **L30 effort words.** No 3-RIR word exists, while 3 RIR is the target for most users (New, Under a year, General). A truthful "Hard" from a lifter at about 2 RIR on a 3-RIR slot counts as an effort miss: two in a row hold the load and slow the cadence. The words carry no rep anchors, so users may read "Hard" as 2–3 reps left.
3. **L42 novice Easy doubling.** A 10% lower-body jump after an all-"Easy" session targeting 3 RIR can turn a true 4-RIR 5-rep set into a roughly 0.5-RIR set next time.
4. **L08 back-offs at 90%** leave about 6 RIR, below the effort and load thresholds. The cost is low for strength (Robinson 2024) but they are wasted minutes in a time-budgeted plan.
5. **L40 and L37** are less harmful than ranked. Step rounding makes 1.25% versus 2.5% moot on most loads. Cadence 2 is ACSM's own rule, and the app's maximum experienced rate (about 2.5%/week lower and 1.25%/week upper at two exposures with shared credit) brackets the 8-week rates of trained men (Helms 2018: about 1.3–1.5%/week squat, about 1.1%/week bench).
6. **L25 Epley** misleads most on the leg press and other high-rep machines, overestimating e1RM by about 15% from a 13-rep set, and on the deadlift, underestimating by 9–14%. Round-tripping on the same exercise limits the error to a few percent of load.

### Gaps
- No user-level data on how often calibration produces a failed set. The app's own logs (calibration sets followed by Max or a rep miss) would answer it.

## Q4. Where this audit confirms or contradicts the first review (2026-11-08 report)

### Takeaway
It confirms the review's core RIR findings and adds the numbers it lacked. It verifies four of its [verify] items (ACSM 2009 2–10%, Grgic 2018, Nuzzo values, Epley's 10-rep limit), contradicts two framings (the absolute-kg asymmetry as grounds for a 2:1 percent increment ratio, and the ACSM 2009 attribution of the 40%-to-2% gains), and qualifies "training status does not matter" for RIR accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- **Confirmed:** Halperin's 0.95-rep underprediction, training status β −0.006, and accuracy better at 12 or fewer reps. This audit adds the between-person SD of 1.45 reps. — [Halperin 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542869/)
- **Confirmed with numbers:** Refalo 2024 raw −0.17 ± 1.00 and absolute 0.65 ± 0.78; Remmert 2023 no effect of sex or experience. — [Refalo 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37967832/); [Remmert 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37036795/)
- **Qualified:** Steele 2017 (n = 141) found that accuracy "tended" to improve with experience. Zourdos 2016 and Ormsbee 2019 found novices under-rate effort at 1RM. So "beginners can use RIR" holds on average, but novices are less reliable near max. — [Steele 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29204323/); [Zourdos 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26049792/); [Ormsbee 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28301439/)
- **Verified [verify] items:**
  - ACSM 2009's increment rule, which also contains the "two consecutive training sessions" condition the review attributed only to NSCA exam prep. — [ACSM 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/)
  - Grgic 2018's >2 min for trained lifters. — [Grgic 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28933024/)
  - Nuzzo's rep values, which the review said not to quote, are now extracted. — [Nuzzo 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933212/)
  - The Epley formula and the ≤10-rep accuracy (Reynolds 2006, Mayhew 2008). — [Reynolds 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16937972/); [Mayhew 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18714230/)
- **Contradicted:** the "about 40% untrained to 2% elite" gains are not in the ACSM 2009 full text. — [ACSM 2009 hosted copy](https://sfu.ca/~ryand/kin343/ACSMresistance.pdf)
- **Contradicted in framing:** Latella's asymmetry is in absolute kg. Helms 2018 shows a smaller *percent* gap between bench and squat in trained men. — [Latella 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35019902/); [Helms 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5877330/)
- **Confirmed:** Rogerson 2024's 6.4 days every 5.6 weeks; Coleman 2024's strength cost of a full-stop deload; Hickmott 2022's MD 2.07 kg (SMD 0.21). — [Rogerson 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38499934/); [Coleman 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38274324/); [Hickmott 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35038063/)
- **Still unverified:** Bosquet's "no loss up to 28 days". The abstract gives only the dose-response and the larger effect over 65. — [Bosquet 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23347054/)

### Inferences
- The review's percent rule ("about 2.5% upper, 5% lower") already scales with load. The app's further halving for experienced lifters (L40) has no support, and its upper value falls below ACSM's 2%.
- The review's "treat a reported 2 RIR as roughly 2 to 3" supports L32's one-rep tolerance. The same logic does *not* support treating "Easy" as roughly 12 RIR, which is what ×1.25 assumes.

### Gaps
- Kraemer & Ratamess 2004 and ACSM 2002, the likely sources of the 40%-to-2% figures, were not checked.
