# Run/walk ladder: what the evidence says about a beginner's first 8 to 10 weeks

Research for the owner decision of 2026-11-17: two Easy sessions per level and a re-stepped table with no weekly
rise in running time above about 20 percent. Written 2026-11-17 against origin/main 7455c807.

Grades: **strong** (several consistent RCTs or meta-analyses), **moderate** (one good RCT or meta-analysis, or
several consistent cohorts), **weak** (one exploratory cohort, small or conflicting studies), **practice** (what
published programs or coaches do, with no outcome test). "Verified" means I read the primary text or its full
abstract this session; "carried" means the 2026-11-08 or 2026-11-13 evidence notes read it and I did not re-read it.

## Summary

- No beginner program, trial or cohort has tested a weekly cap on running time. Every number a ladder uses
  (session length, run/walk ratio, sessions per step, weekly rise) is **practice**.
- The 10 percent rule did not prevent injury in the one RCT that tested it on novices (Buist 2008: 20.8 vs 20.3
  percent). Two exploratory cohorts link bigger weekly jumps (above 20 percent in Damsted 2019, above 30 percent
  over two weeks in Nielsen 2014) with more injuries, each with wide intervals and null results elsewhere. The
  largest cohort (Frandsen 2025) found no week-to-week effect but did find a single-session spike effect. So a
  cap of about 20 percent a week is **weak evidence plus practice**: it is conservative, it is the threshold below
  which Damsted saw fewer early injuries, and it is gentler than any published novice program.
- Published programs climb faster than 20 percent in their worst week: Couch-to-5K +78 percent (week 3 to 4),
  GRONORUN's standard 8-week program +53 percent, its 10%-rule program at most +14 percent. Both GRONORUN arms
  injured about a fifth of runners.
- Two sessions per level is **practice**. Couch-to-5K repeats each step three times a week (27 sessions to 30
  minutes), GRONORUN's standard program runs 22 sessions, its graded one 37, Start to Run about 12 to 18 sessions in
  six weeks. A two-per-level ladder takes 24 Easy sessions, inside that range.
- The beginner risk is real and is the case for caution: novices are injured at about 2.3 times the recreational
  rate per hour (Videbæk 2015, moderate), and most drop-outs from Couch-to-5K blamed injury and fast progression
  (Relph 2023, weak).

## 1. Published beginner programs

**NHS Couch-to-5K, as delivered in Relph 2023** (verified, Table 1). Three runs a week for nine weeks, each after a
5-minute brisk walk. Week 1: 1 min run, 1.5 min walk, ×8. Week 2: 1.5 min run, 2 min walk, ×6. Week 3: (1.5 run,
1.5 walk, 3 run, 3 walk) ×2. Week 4: 3, 5, 3, 5 min runs with 1.5 to 2.5 min walks. Week 5: 5 min ×3, then 8 min
×2, then 20 min continuous. Week 6: 5/8/5, then 10 ×2, then 25 min. Weeks 7 to 9: 25, 28 and 30 min continuous.
Weekly running minutes (my arithmetic): 24, 27, 27, 48, 51, 63, 75, 84, 90. Week-over-week: +12, 0, **+78**, +6,
+24, +19, +12, +7 percent. The NHS page says the plan can take "9 weeks or longer"; it does not say how to repeat.
— [Relph 2023, IJERPH 20:6682, DOI 10.3390/ijerph20176682](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10487403/);
[NHS Couch to 5K](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/). Grade: practice.

**GRONORUN** (verified, protocol Tables 1 and 2). Novices preparing for a 4-mile (6.7 km) event, three sessions a
week, a 5-minute brisk walk before and after, conversational pace. Both arms began with **10 × (1 min run, 1 min
walk)**, 10 minutes of running a session and 30 a week. The standard 8-week program's weekly running minutes: 30,
46, 60, 50, 74, 90, 95 (+53, +30, −17, +48, +22, +6 percent). The graded 13-week program raised running time "10%
per week": 30, 34, 36, 40, 44, 48, 54, 56, 64, 72, 80, 90 (largest step +14 percent). Both end at 30 to 40 minutes
continuous. — [Buist 2007 protocol, BMC Musculoskelet Disord 8:24, PMC1821023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1821023/).
Grade: practice (the programs); the outcome is in section 3.

**Start to Run (Netherlands)** (verified, abstracts). A supervised 6-week course, one group and one or two own
sessions a week, aiming at 20 minutes continuous. NLstart2run: 10.9 percent of 1,696 runners sustained an injury
during the course; older age, higher BMI, earlier non-sport complaints and no running experience raised risk.
Within 26 weeks, 29.5 percent of 774 had stopped running, injury the main reason (48 percent of those who
stopped). — [Kluitenberg 2015, Scand J Med Sci Sports, DOI 10.1111/sms.12346](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25438823/);
[Fokkema 2019, JSAMS 22:106, DOI 10.1016/j.jsams.2018.06.003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29934211/). The exact
weekly schedule was not found. Grade: practice (program), moderate (incidence).

**Vancouver Sun Run "In Training" clinics** (verified, abstract). A 13-week walk/run program toward 10 km, 844
mostly recreational runners: 29.5 percent reported an injury (any pain after running counted); running only one
day a week was a risk factor for women and overall. — [Taunton 2003, BJSM 37:239, DOI 10.1136/bjsm.37.3.239](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1724633/).
Grade: weak (broad injury definition, no exposure time). It bears on one-day-a-week ladders (design §6).

**Couch-to-5K outcomes** (verified). 110 adults (81.8 percent female, mean age 47.1): 27.3 percent completed,
48.2 percent had dropped out by midpoint and 64.5 percent by the end; 74.6 percent of drop-outs left before
midpoint; 21 injuries in 20 runners (19 percent); injury was the reason for 11 of 15 interviewed drop-outs;
previous injury gave OR 7.56 (2.06 to 27.75) for not finishing. A participant on week 5: "Monday you are doing 5
(minutes running), Wednesday you are doing 8 then Friday you are doing 20! And that is a massive leap." Drop-outs
who missed sessions "felt unable to keep up with others in the group upon their return". The authors recommend
"more flexible designs" and "a more tailored and graduated increase in running time and distance, supported by a
longer programme overall". — [Relph 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10487403/). Grade: weak (one
modified-delivery cohort, qualitative attribution), but it is the only drop-out study of the program the ladder
copies.

## 2. Run/walk ratios and session length

- Every published novice program starts with 1-minute runs: 1:1.5 (Couch-to-5K), 1:1 (GRONORUN). First sessions
  carry 8 (Couch-to-5K) or 10 (GRONORUN) minutes of running, 20 to 30 minutes on foot with warm-up walks. All end at
  30 minutes or more continuous. Grade: practice.
- Walk breaks shrink as runs grow: Couch-to-5K's walks run 1.5 to 3 min beside 1.5 to 8 min runs; GRONORUN's 1 to
  5 min beside 1 to 20 min runs. Grade: practice.
- Run/walk lowers perceived fatigue and muscle pain without slowing a marathon (Hottenrott 2016, 42 non-elite
  runners randomized; cardiac biomarkers rose equally; injury not measured). — [Hottenrott 2016, JSAMS, DOI 10.1016/j.jsams.2014.11.010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25467199/).
  Grade: moderate for comfort; no evidence run/walk prevents injury in beginners (no trial found, as the
  2026-11-08 notes also say).
- The longest unbroken run: no study tests how fast it may grow. Relph's drop-outs singled out week 5's bout jump
  (8 to 20 minutes, ×2.5) together with the rising session time. Grade: weak (qualitative).

## 3. How big a weekly step is safe

- **The 10 percent rule failed its trial.** GRONORUN: 532 novices randomized to the standard 8-week program or the
  graded 13-week 10%-rule program; injury (a complaint restricting running for at least a week) 20.3 vs 20.8
  percent, p = .90. — [Buist 2008, Am J Sports Med, DOI 10.1177/0363546507307505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17940147/).
  Verified. Grade: moderate (one RCT, null, novices). It shows a slower start-to-finish program did not cut injury;
  it does not show large weekly jumps are safe (the standard arm's worst week was +53 percent and still injured a
  fifth).
- **Nielsen 2014** (verified abstract): 874 novices with GPS, self-structured running, 1 year. No difference in
  overall injury across <10 percent, 10 to 30 percent and >30 percent weekly progression. For "distance-related"
  injuries, >30 percent vs <10 percent gave HR 1.59 (0.96 to 2.66, p = .07). The authors still advise novices to
  progress "by less than 30% per week over a 2-week period". — [Nielsen 2014, JOSPT 44:739, DOI 10.2519/jospt.2014.5164](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25155475/).
  Grade: weak (exploratory, interval crosses 1).
- **Damsted 2019** (verified abstract): 261 runners over 14 weeks toward a half marathon, 21.5 percent injured. At
  day 21, a 20 to 60 percent weekly rise vs under 20 percent gave a risk difference of 22.6 percent (0.9 to 44.3);
  no difference at days 56 and 98. — [Damsted 2019, JOSPT 49:230, DOI 10.2519/jospt.2019.8541](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30526231/).
  Grade: weak. This is where "about 20 percent" comes from, and it was seen early in a program, which is where the
  ladder sits.
- **Frandsen 2025** (verified abstract; full text carried from the 2026-11-13 audit): 5,205 Garmin runners
  (22 percent female, experienced), 588,071 sessions. Against "regression or up to 10%" over the longest run of the
  past 30 days, single-session spikes of >10 to 30 percent gave HRR 1.64 (1.31 to 2.05), >30 to 100 percent 1.52,
  >100 percent 2.28. "No relationship was identified for the week-to-week" ratio; ACWR showed an inverse
  relation. — [Frandsen 2025, BJSM, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2024-109380](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12421110/).
  Grade: moderate for the session spike (one large exploratory cohort, no novices); against a weekly rule.
- **Run Clever** (verified abstract; schedule carried): 839 recreational runners, 3 runs a week; volume progression
  in 4-week blocks with +23 percent in each block's first week; no injury difference between volume and intensity
  progression (16-week risk difference −14.0 percent, −36.9 to 8.9). — [Ramskov 2018, BMJ Open SEM, DOI 10.1136/bmjsem-2017-000333](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5841490/).
  Grade: weak for this question (not novices, not a test of the rise itself).
- **Reviews.** Nielsen 2012 (31 studies, mean quality 44 percent): "not possible to identify which training errors
  were related to running related injuries" — [IJSPT, PMC3290924](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3290924/).
  Damsted 2018 calls the evidence that sudden load changes cause injury "very limited" (carried) —
  [IJSPT, PMC6253751](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6253751). Fredette 2022 (36 studies, 23,047 runners;
  novices 14.9 percent injured): evidence on weekly distance, duration, frequency, intensity "or specific changes in
  training parameters" was conflicting, so "caution should be taken when recommending optimal parameters or
  progressions". — [J Athl Train 57:650, DOI 10.4085/1062-6050-0195.21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9528699/).
  Verified. Grade: moderate that no specific weekly threshold is established.
- **Why caution anyway.** Injury is the load exceeding a tissue's capacity in a session or across sessions
  (Bertelsen 2017's framework, practice/theory — [Scand J Med Sci Sports, DOI 10.1111/sms.12883](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28329441/)),
  and novices have the least capacity: 17.8 (16.7 to 19.1) vs 7.7 (6.9 to 8.7) injuries per 1,000 h for novice vs
  recreational runners — [Videbæk 2015, Sports Med, DOI 10.1007/s40279-015-0333-8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4473093/).
  Verified. Grade: moderate. A 4-week walking and hopping preconditioning block did not lower injuries in novices
  (15.2 vs 16.8 percent) — [Bredeweg 2012, BJSM, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2012-091397](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22842237/).
  Verified. Grade: moderate (one RCT).

## 4. Sessions per level

- No study compares advancing after one, two or three sessions at a step, or by rating instead of by calendar
  week. The 2026-11-13 audit found none either. Grade: no evidence.
- Published programs repeat each step about three times (Couch-to-5K weeks 1 to 4 and 7 to 9 run one session
  three times; GRONORUN repeats or varies within a week). Community guidance for Couch-to-5K is to repeat a week that
  felt hard; no primary source states it. Grade: practice.
- Counts of sessions to 30 minutes continuous: Couch-to-5K 27; GRONORUN standard 22 (to its 30 to 40 minute runs),
  graded 37; Start to Run about 12 to 18 (to 20 minutes). The current ladder's 12 is below all of them; 24 (two per
  level) is inside the range. Grade: practice.

## 5. What this means for the ladder (the design's evidence column)

| Rule | Grade | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Two Easy sessions per level, any order, a Good or unrated session keeps the credit | practice (no injury or drop-out benefit shown) | Published programs repeat each step about three times; 24 sessions sit inside 22 to 37; R43's effort gate is kept |
| Weekly running time rises at most about 20 percent at the planned cadence | weak + practice | Damsted 2019 (<20 percent at day 21); Nielsen 2014 (<30 percent over two weeks, novices); null RCT for 10 percent (Buist 2008); reviews find no established threshold (Fredette 2022) |
| Level 1 = 10 × (1 min run, 1.5 min walk) | practice (owner 2026-11-17) | GRONORUN week 1 in both RCT arms (10 × 1 min run, 1 min walk); Couch-to-5K week 1 is 8 × (1 min run, 1.5 min walk), the same walk; no study compares the two doses |
| Level 12 = 30 min continuous, two Easy finishes | practice | Couch-to-5K's endpoint (R38 kept); GRONORUN ends at 30 to 40 min |
| Each new level ≤ +12.5 percent running time over the level before | practice, guided by Frandsen | Frandsen's reference band is "up to 10%" by distance; the ladder steps 6.7 to 12.5 percent by time (an extrapolation; Frandsen had no novices) |
| Hard or Pain drops one level and clears the credit | practice | Audit 2026-11-13 row 3; Relph: previous injury OR 7.56 |
| A one-day-a-week ladder (24 weeks) | weak caution | Taunton 2003: one day a week was a risk factor |

## Gaps

- No novice study measures weekly running time (all use distance), so applying their percentages to time is an
  extrapolation; at a beginner's steady easy pace the two move together.
- The Start to Run week-by-week schedule and the ACSM guidelines' minutes-per-week progression advice were not
  found in a primary source; neither is used.
- Nothing tests a transition from intervals to one continuous run; the 2.25× bout step in the design (12 → 27 min
  at an unchanged 27 minutes on foot) is practice, with no shown injury or drop-out benefit.
- Nothing tests a cap on counted sessions a week or a minimum time between level-ups. The owner's 3 counted sessions
  per rolling 7 days follows Couch-to-5K's and GRONORUN's three sessions a week (practice), and the design's level-up
  guard (design.md Revision 3 §2.3) is practice sized by simulation, not by an outcome study.
